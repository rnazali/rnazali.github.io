title: The Weaver
---

The threads on the loom made no sense to Elian.

From where he sat on the dusty floor, looking up at the bottom of the frame, the tapestry was a chaotic mess. There were jagged lines of dark charcoal wool, knots of harsh crimson, and long patches of empty gray.

"It is ugly," Elian whispered, looking at his calloused hands. "The dark threads hurt. They feel like the winter that took my crops. Why did you use so much black here?"

The Weaver sat above the frame, hands moving with steady, rhythmic grace. The shuttle slid through the warp like a breath. Click-clack. Click-clack.

"You are looking at the underside, little one," the Weaver said, voice sounding like wind through dry leaves. "You see the knots and the tangled ends. You see the back of the work."

"Will it ever be beautiful?" Elian asked, squeezing his eyes shut against a rising tear. "I do not understand the pattern."

"You cannot read it yet," the Weaver replied softly. "But I do not throw a single thread by accident. I weave the scorching sun that parches your field, and I weave the gentle rain that coaxes the seed. Both are needed for the harvest."

The Weaver reached down, gently lifting Elian up to stand on the stool beside the loom.

From this new vantage point, Elian looked down at the top of the tapestry. His breath caught.

The harsh crimson threads he feared were actually the vibrant petals of a sunrise. The dark charcoal lines were not shadows of ruin, but the rich, deep soil from which a field of golden wheat was bursting forth. The empty gray was a morning mist, making the light look soft and holy.

It was a masterpiece of growth, survival, and overwhelming beauty.

"I see it now," Elian whispered.

The Weaver smiled, turning back to the loom, picking up a bright thread of pure gold. "Not all of it, my child. The story is still long, and the shuttle must keep moving. Trust the hand that holds the thread."

God writes a story I cannot yet read. I trust the weaver of the sun and rain.
